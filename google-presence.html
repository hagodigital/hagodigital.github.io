01

The profile, filled in properly

Every field, not just the obvious ones

Most profiles are half done — a name, a phone number and one photo from four years ago. I go through the whole thing: the categories that decide what you turn up for, the services written out, the attributes, the description, the photos.

  • Primary and secondary categories chosen deliberately, not guessed
  • Services, description and attributes written out in full
  • Photos organised, and the ones working against you taken down
  • Your name, address and phone identical to your site, character for character

One thing we won’t promise: how long Google takes to verify a profile. Google controls verification, moderation and ranking — not us. We prepare the profile correctly and guide the process, but nobody can override Google’s decisions or guarantee a position in search results, and anyone quoting you a date is guessing.

02

Pointing at everything else you own

A profile on its own is a dead end

The listing is where people arrive. It has to send them somewhere — to the site, to a message, or straight into your diary. Set up alongside a build, the profile and the site point at each other and carry the same details.

  • Linked to your website, and your website linked back
  • A booking link on your own Google Calendar
  • The contact routes you actually answer, and none you don’t
  • Everything that changes — hours, prices, photos — lives here, where you can edit it free

That last one is deliberate. Anything with an expiry date belongs on the profile, not baked into a website you’d have to pay someone to change.

03

Reviews, asked for on purpose

The asking is the part that breaks down

Nearly everyone means to ask and forgets, or asks awkwardly at the wrong moment. You leave with a one-tap review link off your own profile and a plain routine for when to send it — so asking stops depending on remembering.

  • A direct review link that opens straight on the form
  • Where to put it — the invoice, the follow-up message, the sign-off
  • How to reply to the ones you get, including the bad one
  • Written into the manual, so it survives after I’m gone

What I can set up is the asking. Whether the reviews come is down to the work you do and the customers who write them — I’m not going to pretend otherwise.
