About

Hago. Spanish for “I make.”

Not a slogan — a way of working.

I started HagoDigital because of a pattern I kept noticing: the best business in a suburb is very often not the one getting the work. Someone with real skill, a decade of it, losing jobs to a competitor who is simply easier to find and easier to trust at a glance. That gap is unfair, and it’s fixable, and closing it is a genuinely interesting problem.

Hago is Spanish for “I make.” There’s a difference between consuming and making — consuming takes what already exists; making is the slower business of building something that wasn’t there before. That’s the difference I want this to be built on.

It’s also why I tend to get on with the people I work with: you’re a maker too. You didn’t just imagine a business, you started one, and most people never get that far. So we start on common ground — you made something worth choosing, and my job is making sure the right people can tell.

Marketing has been a long-running interest rather than a career I fell into. What I find compelling is the honest version of the problem: how a good business earns attention, trust, and a decision, without exaggerating and without tricks. I’d rather show you one specific thing that’s wrong with your Google listing than tell you that you don’t know what you don’t know.

When you work with HagoDigital you work with me. Not an account manager, not a team you never meet. That’s a real limit as much as a feature — I can only take on so much at once, and I’d rather say that plainly than pretend otherwise.

That limit is also the reason to pick me. There is no team to hide behind, so your website is my portfolio and your Google listing is my reference: I only win when you do. You don’t need someone who overpromises and underdelivers. You need someone who treats your business as though their reputation depends on it. Mine does.

— JP, founder
