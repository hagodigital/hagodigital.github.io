The gap

Being good at the job and looking good at it are two different skills.

Nobody told you that when you started. You learned a trade, or a craft, or a practice — and you got properly good at it. Then you found out that customers can’t assess how good you are. They can only assess what they can see. A Google listing with no photos and the wrong hours. A website that looks like it was built in 2014, because it was. So they pick the one that looks sorted, and you never find out you were in the running. That’s not a marketing problem. It’s a recognition problem, and it’s the one I work on.

Who it’s for

What I do

Two things. Both yours to keep.

The list is deliberately short. I’d rather do two things properly than six things adequately, and telling you what I don’t do is faster than scoping it later.

Get found  →  get chosen

Google Business Profile

The listing that decides whether you turn up in Maps and local search at all. Categories, services, hours, photos, the description, and the review habit that keeps it moving. It’s a free channel and most businesses leave it half-built — which is exactly why it’s the first thing I fix.

A static website

Fast, built by hand, no database and no monthly hosting bill. Someone finds you on Google, lands here, and decides. Every page has a job and a reason to exist — I don’t build pages nothing sends traffic to.

Attaches to a website build

Brand Basics

Colours that actually pass contrast, a type pairing, and a wordmark. Not a separate project — it gets made during the site build either way, so you may as well have it written down. Full vector, print and signage work goes to a proper designer.

Want ads as well? Meta and Google Ads are built and managed by hand — but only when you ask, and only once there’s something worth sending traffic to. It isn’t the default and I won’t push it on you.
